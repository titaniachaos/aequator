# The Äquatormaßband comes to school

<p class="chronicle-intro">We collect, sew, play and tell stories. Here we document what happens during our visits – with fabrics, stories and the young people who help shape the ribbon.</p>

Bianca Trappl and Titania Chaos are working with two classes throughout the school year. Our shared goal: to let the ribbon grow and, at the end of the year, symbolically wrap it around the school building. How we get there will develop as we meet.

We first met one of the classes on 10 and 17 June 2026 – in the park outside the school and at the school itself. This chronicle follows the 2026/27 school year.

## 21 October 2026 · Many languages and a thread of patience {#besuch-2026-10-21}

<p class="chronicle-byline">A report by Titania Chaos</p>

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-10-21-klassenzimmer" lang="en" :width="960" :height="720" />

A ball made from my clown scarves got us started. We threw it to one another, saying our own names and the name of the person we were throwing to. A simple social circus game about getting to know one another, but also about paying attention to each other. Then we brought in the languages spoken in the group: Serbian, Bosnian, Croatian, Kurdish, Turkish, Russian and Ukrainian. In the final round, something said in another language travelled through the classroom along with the ball.

We had already met the twelve young people twice before the summer. Together, we recalled what the Äquatormaßband project is about. We unrolled lengths of fabric ribbon from the cable reels and looked at what other people had already contributed: different fabrics, materials and stories. One student returned to the sewing he had started on our previous visit. Another had brought fabric and added 150 centimetres to the ribbon. She told us she had been looking forward to our visit because she wanted to support the project.

Bianca and I played with our roles. I acted up; she brought me back into line as “the boss”. In between, we talked about fabrics, how they are made, and words that were unfamiliar to some of us. We agreed to ask whenever we did not know a word. “Petrol”, the colour, needed explaining too.

Some of the young people tried knotting wool with their fingers. Bianca noted down names and stories so we could pick up where we left off next time. I tied a white “thread of patience” around everyone’s arm. As I did, we got talking.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-10-21-fingerknuepfen" lang="en" :width="720" :height="960" />

Our two-hour visit ended with a spontaneous stop in another classroom. There, we had about ten minutes to introduce the Äquatormaßband to eight or nine young people.

Next time, we would like to make more room for the young people’s own ideas. What fabrics they will bring, what stories they will tell, and how they will help shape the ribbon is the most exciting part.
