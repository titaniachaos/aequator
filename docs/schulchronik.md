# The Äquatormaßband comes to school

<p class="chronicle-intro">We collect, sew, play and tell stories. Here we document what happens during our visits – with fabrics, stories and the young people who help shape the ribbon.</p>

Bianca Trappl and Titania Chaos are working with two classes throughout the school year. Our shared goal: to let the ribbon grow and, at the end of the year, symbolically wrap it around the school building. How we get there will develop as we meet.

We first met one of the classes on 10 and 17 June 2026 – in the park outside the school and at the school itself. This chronicle follows the 2026/27 school year.

## 11 November 2026 · Project Plans, a Circle of Chairs and a Circle Skirt {#besuch-2026-11-11}

<p class="chronicle-byline">A report by Titania Chaos</p>

When we came into the classroom this time, the chairs were already arranged in a circle. The students had set them up themselves after we had talked about it at our last meeting. A small change we were pleased to see.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-11-11-stuhlkreis" lang="en" :width="960" :height="653" />

We began by looking at what it takes to get a project like the Äquatormaßband off the ground. You have an idea, think about what you want to achieve, make plans, find people to work with and set about putting it into practice. And then quite a few things usually turn out differently than planned.

We also talked about education. The more you know and can do, the more choices you have in shaping your own life. We discussed how education can help people overcome poverty, which brought us to [Nelson Mandela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Mandela). Nobody in the class knew who he was, so we told them a little about him.

We had also brought new fabrics that had been added at other events over the previous two weeks. One piece came from the wedding dress of a woman from Somalia. We looked at the fabrics and talked about where they came from and the stories behind them.

Then another class came to visit. About eight students joined us with their teacher and learned about the Äquatormaßband. One student took on the task of passing the website link on to the others. An article for the school newspaper is also planned.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-11-11-schulzeitung" lang="en" :width="960" :height="797" />

At our last meeting, one student had mentioned that she might like to sew a circle skirt herself. This time, we had brought one along. I showed how the fabric spreads out when you spin, and how spinning works. Along the way, we learned a new term: the [vestibular system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestibular_system).

Then the student put on a circle skirt herself and spun in front of the class.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-11-11-kreisrock-video" lang="en" playsinline autoplay-visible :width="720" :height="1280" />

Towards the end, we discussed the next steps. The students would also like to do something together outside school. A possible visit to the Weltmuseum Wien is being considered. We also talked about the coming dates and how we might encircle the school building with the Äquatormaßband at the end of the school year.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-11-11-zusammenarbeit" lang="en" :width="720" :height="960" />

So there is plenty to plan. And, as we now know, probably not everything will go according to plan.

## 21 October 2026 · The Äquatormaßband goes to school {#besuch-2026-10-21}

<p class="chronicle-byline">A report by Titania Chaos</p>

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-10-21-klassenzimmer" lang="en" :width="960" :height="720" />

After our two meetings in June, Bianca and I returned to the Berufsschule für Handel und Reisen in Vienna. We are continuing to work with the same class during the 2026/27 school year. The young people are now, so to speak, the head of the Equator at school. From here, we want the project to spread to other classes too.

We began with a ball I had made by tying my clown scarves together. We threw it to one another, saying our own names and the name of the person we were throwing to. The game comes from social circus: you get to know one another while having to pay attention to each other. Then we wanted to find out which languages besides German were spoken in the class. There were quite a few: Serbian, Bosnian, Croatian, Kurdish, Turkish, Russian and Ukrainian. Finally, everyone had a chance to say something in another language too.

We unwound the fabric we had already collected from the cable reels, looked at the different materials and talked about where they come from and how they are made. One student continued the sewing he had started. Another had even brought a piece of fabric and told us its story. She had been looking forward to our visit and wanted to contribute something to the project. Her contribution made the Äquatormaßband 150 centimetres longer.

In between, we played, asked questions and explained things. Not every word was familiar to everyone. What, for instance, is “[petrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum)”? We agreed it was better to ask whenever something was unclear.

Bianca showed some of the young people how to knot wool with their fingers. She also noted down names and some of what was said so we could pick up on it next time. I tied a white “thread of patience” around everyone’s wrist. That gave me a chance to talk with each person a little more personally.

<MediaFigure id="aequator-schule-2026-10-21-fingerknuepfen" lang="en" :width="720" :height="960" />

After two hours, we made an impromptu visit to another class. There, we spent about ten minutes introducing the Äquatormaßband.

At our next meetings, we want above all to give the young people more room to speak for themselves. We are curious to see what fabrics and stories they will bring, and what ideas they will have for our shared project.
